THE FIELD MANUAL

Chess, dealt differently.

  1. Choose one of your three cards. Both players’ hands are public; the shuffled draw pile is hidden.
  2. Use its power—or discard it. A power replaces your ordinary move. To move normally, choose “Discard & make a normal move.” Either choice spends one card.
  3. Select a piece and a highlighted square. For a shield or freeze, just select the target piece. A swap selects two friendly pieces.
  4. Draw back to three automatically. The replacement is drawn when your action finishes and is available on your next turn. There is no extra draw on the opening turn.

Temporary powers, standard pieces

A movement card lasts for that action only. A pawn moved like a rook is still a pawn afterward. All leaps ignore intervening pieces; slides require a clear path unless the card explicitly says otherwise. Pawns reaching the opposite back rank promote normally. Kings never receive card powers, cannot be frozen or shielded, and can never be captured.

Check still matters

A card can help escape check, but no action may leave your king attacked by the opponent’s normal piece moves. Held cards do not themselves give check. Frozen pieces still attack; freezing a checking piece does not end the check. Checkmate considers every legal action in your current hand, including discarding for a normal move.

Lasting effects

Glass Aegis prevents capture for one opposing turn. Winter Seal prevents a piece moving for its next turn. Both still block paths and attack normally; effects expire after that turn, even if the player uses another effect card.

Familiar rules and controls

Castling, en passant, promotion and the 50-move draw remain. Repetition compares the full position, including hands and deck state. In card mode only bare kings automatically draw for insufficient material. Use 2D for keyboard play: Tab or arrows to focus, Enter to select. Undo restores the exact hands and draw pile, as well as the board.

Your match saves locally. This edition starts a fresh card game; old hero saves are kept separately. Inspired by the tradition of card-chess games such as Knightmare Chess; these 18 cards use original names and wording.
